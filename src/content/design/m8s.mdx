## Context

m8s (may-ts) was a group project for the Oasis project series at Northeastern. The brief was open: build something full-stack that solves a real problem. Every one of us lived with roommates, and we all had the same complaint.

Chore charts on the fridge become redundant in a week. Group chats bury the reminder under everything else. The result is a quiet ledger of resentment that nobody wants to bring up.

## Research

We talked to roommates across a handful of households and asked them to walk us through the last chore argument they had. Combined with our own personal experiences living with roommates and the pattern revealed itself:

<Quote cite="Anonymous Student">I think taking out the trash or doing dishes isn't that bad. It's just annoying if someone keeps forgetting to do it and I have to do it for them.</Quote>

Fairness turned out to be about visibility: people were fine with uneven weeks as long as everyone could see the balance evening out over time.

## Define

Four insights shaped every screen that followed.

<Cards
  items={[
    { title: 'Fairness is perceived', text: 'People judged fairness by effort and visibility, not by raw task count.' },

    { title: 'Schedules drift', text: 'Rotations broke the first week someone travelled, so swapping had to be a first-class action.' },

## Design

The wireframes and in-progress Figma files from this phase weren't kept, so this section covers the direction rather than the artifacts. The insights above set the priorities: a shared, glanceable view of the household's week, reminders that come from the app instead of a roommate, and swapping as a normal action rather than a workaround.

The visual language was kept deliberately small, so a group of developers could apply it consistently without a designer reviewing every screen.

## Outcome

While the app was not fully functional by the end of the series, accounts, households, rotating chores and swaps were all built out along with the backend database and primary dashboard UIs.

## Reflection

m8s works as a semester project, but the parts that would make it last are research, documentation and a platform people actually carry around. The web build already scales down to a phone width, but a responsive site isn't the same as a mobile interface. If I took it further, I'd start here.
