## Context

Sandbox's Tadpole design challenge asked for a redesign of Northeastern's Library Calendar, the tool students use to find and book rooms in Snell Library. The brief offered four directions: rethink the availability calendar, rethink the booking flow, fix accessibility pain points, or add features. I took on the first two together, since finding a room and booking it are one task for the person doing it.

The challenge was scoped to about five hours and graded on reasoning more than polish: research, ideation, then wireframes, with notes explaining each decision.

<Callout>How might we get a busy student from "I need a room" to a confirmed booking in as few steps as possible?</Callout>

## Research

I started by booking a room on the live tool and annotating every point of friction.

A few problems stood out:

- The calendar is a huge wall of time slots, and the colour key that explains it sits at the bottom of the page.
- "Seat Style" is loosely defined, and the collapsing menu repeats the same filter.
- A full month view doesn't fit how students book: most need a room today or tomorrow.
- The booking details page adds friction, "Continue" leads to yet another menu, and a booking can't be edited once it's confirmed.
- Confirmed bookings are hard to find, and the list is hard to scan.

For inspiration I looked at three products. **Northeastern's own homepage** offered high contrast, bold type and a modified shape language to bring in. **Squire**, a booking tool for barbers, has a fast linear flow with a running receipt beside it. A barbershop client and a student booking a room share the same job: reserve a block of time at short notice, quickly. **Wanderu** filters out what isn't available and puts the best options on top, which is exactly what a room list needs.

## Design

The guiding rule was to reduce how much a student has to think. The default view assumes the common case (solo, studying, any open room as soon as possible), so most people never touch a filter.

<Figure src="/Assets/design/uiux/nu-library/cover.webp" alt="Redesigned browse view with filter sidebar and three matching rooms" ratio="1633 / 1162" caption="Browse: a filter sidebar, active-filter chips, and each room's day as one timeline." />

### A sidebar for the student who needs a room now

Most students are looking to book now and will take anything open, so filters live in a sidebar alongside the results instead of in a form you fill out first. Quick-search tags handle the big cuts, and each listing shows a summary with its open slots inside your window.

Results sort by what matters most when you're in a hurry:

1. Open time slots closest to the current time and date
2. Duration, up to the maximum reservation length
3. Larger capacity and accessible seating
4. Whiteboards, TVs, outlets and other features
5. Location

### Booking without the detours

Picking a room opens a detail card with photos, features and a draggable time range. **Quick book** goes straight to the library's single sign-on and confirms. **Add to list** doesn't require logging in, but a banner explains that saved bookings need an account to keep.
