§01

Why it reads cartoon, not instrument

9 findings

The bones are right (zero radius, black blocks, amber, mono microtype). What tips it into "toy" is how things move and how loud every element is. Premium hardware UIs are quiet until touched, use the accent as a signal, and get their character from fine detail (hairlines, numbering, units) instead of big shapes and bounce. Bars = severity.

Overshoot is the theme's default ease

--theme-motion-ease: var(--ease-aggressive) = cubic-bezier(.4, 2, .6, 1). That y1 = 2 overshoots by ~25% before settling, on the CTA wipe, chevron, card info fades, settings toggles and footer. It's the single biggest "rubbery" tell.

SEE PROJECTS← hover

FIXRetire overshoot from engineering. Use one decel curve (cubic-bezier(.2,0,0,1)) at 90–280ms. Machines stop; they don't wobble.

Things move when nobody touched them

Infinite chevron wave bob, dot field with barrel lens + cursor parallax + amber trails, per-letter spring rise on the wordmark, tabs flying in. The page performs at you before you've asked it anything.

⌄infinite bob + scale 1.12

FIXStill at rest. One intro (a wipe, not a bounce), then motion only as feedback to input. Dot field: drop lens + parallax; keep a static grid or a faint scanline.

Syne 800 is a personality font

Syne's wide, quirky geometry in heavy weight, set in amber for project titles, vertical spines and "Let's Build Cool Stuff!", reads like a poster or game menu. It's doing the job the content should do.

PalmerMACROPAD

FIXA neutral expanded grotesk (Archivo at wdth 112–125, already loaded) at 500–600 weight, in ink, not amber. Keep Syne for the wordmark at most.

Amber is everywhere, so it signals nothing

Badge, titles, eyebrows, spines, chip, three different wipe fills, nav fill, pull tab, plus a full-bleed amber footer. On hardware, the amber thing is the thing that's on.

vs

FIXAmber ≤ 3% of any screen: LEDs, the active index, focus ring, one highlight. Footer becomes a dark panel with one lit key.

Soft blurred shadows on hard-edged cards

0 14px 38px -10px rgba(30,30,20,.4) under zero-radius panels makes them float like paper cut-outs or stickers. Physical instruments are flush. Depth comes from tone and edges.

→

FIXNo blur shadows. Hairline frame + offset registration line, or a 1–2px bevel (inset 0 -2px) for pressable keys only.

No fine-detail layer

Every element is a big solid block at the same weight (crumbs, CTA, nav, logo, toggles). There's nothing small and precise: no index numbers, counts, units, ticks or hairlines. Detail density is what makes gear look expensive.

CH 02 / 04 · 2024 → NOW · ESP32 · 4×SERVO

FIXAdd a microtype tier: mono 10–11px, tracked, muted. Numbered projects (01–04), years as ranges, a ruler or tick row as a structural device.

Geometry and type aren't one system

Pull tabs have 10px radii on a 0-radius page. Four families compete: Syne, Roboto (not actually loaded, so it falls back to Arial), interstate-mono and the card caption mono.

DESIGN10px radius

FIXTwo families (grotesk + mono). One radius token: 0 or 2px everywhere. Tabs become flat side-mounted switches.

Squash-and-scale hovers

Chevron scale(1.12), logo scale(1.05), media scale(1.04), chip scale .9 → 1. Scaling on hover is a squishy, cartoon idiom.

HOVER = STATE

FIXHover changes state, not size: LED on, label goes ink, hairline darkens, readout updates. Press = 1px travel.

The project picker fights the user

The accordion moves the targets under the cursor, collapses titles into vertical slivers, and only shows info on hover, so there's no persistent selection or overview. It also looks like a streaming-app shelf (photo, gradient scrim, colored title).

→ see /prototypes/eng-explorers.html

FIXFixed hit targets, an index you can read at rest, click/keys to select, and a stable viewport that updates in place.
